# 109 — Generate the how-it-works clips

**What to build:** The seven short scene clips that ticket 107's walkthrough page scrubs as the Buyer scrolls,
generated by the operator in Higgsfield (or a similar AI video tool), one clip per step. The clips set the scene
around the product; the real product screen sits on top of or beside each clip. A clip never shows a product
screen of its own, because an invented screen would promise features the product does not have. Operator request
on 2026-10-10.

**Blocked by:** none. This is operator work. Ticket 107 builds the page with still placeholders and does not wait
for it; this ticket's clips replace the placeholders when they are ready.

**Status:** open — operator action. An agent encodes and integrates the clips once they are delivered.

## Before generating

- Confirm that the Higgsfield plan used allows commercial use of its output, and record the plan name, the
  terms page and the date checked in this ticket.
- Every clip must have no real dealership, brand or logo (including on cars), no licence plate, no readable
  text, and no recognisable face. Show people from behind, as hands, or out of focus. A device screen faces
  away from the camera or shows only a soft blank glow.

## Specification for every clip

- One continuous shot of 4 to 6 seconds, with no cuts, and a slow camera move (push-in, slow pan or slow
  dolly). Fast motion looks jittery when it is scrubbed by scroll.
- 16:9, at least 1920 by 1080, 24 frames per second. Keep the subject inside the centre third of the frame so
  a tall phone crop still reads at 360 px.
- Leave one calm area (a plain desk, wall or soft background), on the side named in the table, for the
  product screen to sit over.
- Use the same look in all seven: warm, neutral, evening light, low contrast, so a product screen on top stays
  readable in both light and dark themes.
- Export the highest quality the tool offers, and pick one still frame from each clip as its poster. Deliver
  the raw files outside the repository; the integrating agent compresses them.

## The seven clips

| Step | Scene | Camera | Space for the screen |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Describe the car you want, or start from a quote | A Buyer at a kitchen table in the evening, laptop open with its screen facing away, a mug beside it | Slow push-in | Right half |
| 2. Ask dealers for an out-the-door quote from your own email | Over the shoulder, hands typing on the laptop, the screen a soft blank glow | Slow sideways move | Left half |
| 3. Upload or photograph the dealer's paperwork | Top-down: a phone held above printed paperwork on the table, the text blurred and unreadable | Slow descent | Right half |
| 4. Confirm each number against the outlined spot | Close-up: a finger tracing one line of a worksheet beside a phone, shallow focus | Slow focus pull | Left half |
| 5. Read the report: the total, what stands out, what to ask | The Buyer on a couch reading a phone, calm, seen from the side and behind | Slow arc | Right half |
| 6. Reply to the dealer with a draft you edit and send yourself | Hands holding a phone, a thumb resting near the bottom edge, no interface visible | Slow push-in | Left half |
| 7. At the dealership: photograph the four-square | A plain showroom desk with a four-square sheet on it, a phone raised over it, a salesperson out of focus with their back turned, no badges on the cars | Slow dolly | Right half |

A starting prompt for step 1, to adapt for the others: "Cinematic, warm evening light, a person seen from behind
at a kitchen table with an open laptop whose screen faces away from the camera, a coffee mug, shallow depth of
field, slow push-in, no text, no logos, no faces, 5 seconds, 16:9."
