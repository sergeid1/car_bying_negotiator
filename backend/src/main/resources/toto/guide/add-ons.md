Add-ons are products or services added to the sale. Dealers name the same add-on in many ways, so otdog recognises many names, abbreviations and spellings for each one, such as "Paint Prot.", "Etch", "N2 Tires" or "Ext. Warranty". Each named add-on comes with what it is, whether it is usually optional, and a question to ask.

Pre-installed protection includes paint sealant or film, fabric or interior protection, undercoating or rust proofing, ceramic coating, and appearance, environmental or protection packages. Theft deterrents include VIN or window etching, theft protection or registration, alarms, and tracking or recovery devices. Small accessories priced as extras include nitrogen-filled tires, wheel locks, mud flaps or splash guards, door edge guards, pinstripes, window tint, floor mats and cargo trays.

These are usually optional and negotiable. You can ask whether the add-on is already on the car, whether it is required to buy this vehicle, and what the out-the-door total is without it.

Products sold in the finance office include service contracts or extended warranties, GAP coverage, tire and wheel protection, key replacement, dent and ding protection, windshield protection, maintenance plans, and credit life or disability insurance. Each is an optional product: you can decline it or buy it elsewhere. GAP coverage and credit insurance cover a loan, so on a cash purchase you can ask what they would cover.

An add-on that is financed with the car costs more than its cash price, because you pay interest on it for the whole loan term. The report's Dealer financing section shows that cost for each financed add-on.

When a name only suggests an add-on, otdog shows it as possibly that add-on, with a question. A dealer charge otdog does not recognise, such as a package with a name the dealer made up, is listed as an unrecognised charge, so you can ask what it is. otdog never hides a charge it cannot name.
