In Florida, Texas and North Carolina, a trade-in allowance on the dealer's paperwork lowers the taxable price of the car you are buying. Selling the car elsewhere gives up that tax saving.

In the examples otdog uses, trading in a $12,000 car instead of selling it elsewhere keeps $750.00 of Texas tax and $360.00 of North Carolina tax.

In Texas, the credit applies when the dealer selling you the car takes the trade-in in the same transaction. There is no credit for a car you sell privately or to a different dealer.

In Florida, the allowance comes off before both the state tax and the county surtax. Because the surtax applies only to the first $5,000, a trade-in on a car whose taxable price stays above $5,000 removes state tax but no county surtax.
